Abstract

Multi-robot systems have been widely deployed in real-world applications. Large language models (LLMs) enable multi-robot collaboration without task-specific training. However, most existing LLM-empowered approaches remain inefficient: when confronted with identical or similar tasks, they must replan from scratch because they overlook task-level similarities. Caching solved tasks and reusing their plans (i.e., memoization) can remove this redundancy. Yet identifying and reusing solutions for similar but not identical tasks is nontrivial. The difficulty is that multi-robot planning is hierarchical. The reusability of high-level task decision-making and low-level motion planning depends on task similarity at different granularities. We therefore propose MeCo-Hi, a framework that aligns task similarity with this hierarchy. At the high level, scene similarity compares attributed task graphs via the Fused Gromov–Wasserstein distance. At the low level, trajectory similarity measures how transferable cached trajectories are to the current environment. We further explore the relationship between the two and find that scene similarity is a reliable prior for trajectory similarity, making coarse-to-fine retrieval efficient. We also design a similar motion planner that transfers the retrieved plan at both levels, enabling effective plan reuse without re-invoking LLMs. For better evaluation, we extend MeCoBench with three new categories of task scenarios. Experimental results show that MeCo-Hi substantially reduces planning costs and improves success rates compared with state-of-the-art approaches.

Tasks of the same type share their objective, robots, and constraints, while target objects and obstacle layouts can vary. The extended MeCoBench uses scene similarity to compare each new task with the cache of successfully executed tasks, using a common measure across all nine tasks. It supports three settings:

  • Random (S1): the layout of a new task is randomly generated without any constraint on its scene similarity to the cached tasks, emulating practical applications where similar and dissimilar tasks arrive in a mixed manner.
  • Similar (S2): a new task must be similar to at least one cached task, i.e., there exists Tk∈ℳ such that Simsc(T,Tk)≥τsim. This setting examines the benefit a method can obtain by fully exploiting task similarity.
  • Different (S3): a new task is dissimilar to all cached tasks, i.e., Simsc(T,Tk)<τsim for every Tk∈ℳ. This setting examines whether a method, in the absence of any reusable task, can promptly recognize this and turn to the LLMs for planning without introducing noticeable overhead.

The three settings span situations from fully reusable to non-reusable, allowing a comprehensive evaluation of the effectiveness and efficiency of a method under different degrees of similarity.

MeCo-Hi

Hierarchical retrieval, S-Planner, validation, execution, and selective caching.

We propose MeCo-Hi, a hierarchical similarity-aware multi-robot collaboration framework. When a new task arrives, MeCo-Hi first searches for a similar task in the task cache ℳ. The search proceeds hierarchically, following the correlation between the two levels of similarity. Scene similarity is evaluated over the whole cache to select a candidate set. Trajectory similarity is then computed only for these candidates, among which the one with the highest trajectory similarity is taken as the similar task. Since the expensive computation of trajectory similarity is restricted to a few candidates, the retrieval cost grows slowly as the cache grows.

If no similar task is found, the LLMs plan the task from its description. Otherwise, the similar motion planner, namely S-Planner, plans the current task by reusing the plan of the similar task without invoking the LLMs. At the high level, S-Planner transfers the action sequence and the robot assignment of the similar task according to target re-matching. At the low level, it extracts the valid segments of the reference trajectories to guide motion planning. In either case, the task is decomposed into subtasks, and each subtask is assigned a 3D waypoint path. Every generated plan is then validated by inverse kinematics and collision checking. If the validation fails, the failure reason is fed back to the LLMs for replanning. In particular, when a plan produced by S-Planner fails, the continuous planning module lets the LLMs resume from the failed step rather than restart from scratch, so that the effort already spent on reuse is preserved. Once all validations are passed, the low-level motion planner interpolates the waypoints into trajectories, which are executed by the robots in the environment.

After a task is successfully executed, the selective caching module decides whether to store it in ℳ. Only plans that meet the execution-efficiency requirement are admitted, which prevents redundant steps from propagating to subsequent tasks. Under a bounded capacity, frequently referenced tasks are retained, which keeps the cache compact while preserving its diversity. MeCo-Hi thereby forms a retrieval–reuse–caching loop, in which every solved task makes the next similar task cheaper to plan. As the loop wraps around the planning process rather than replacing it, MeCo-Hi integrates with existing LLM-empowered multi-robot methods in a plug-and-play manner.

Comparison with MeCo

With RoCo as the planner and DeepSeek-v4-flash as the LLM, MeCo-Hi raises the average success rate across five tasks from 69.2% to 80.8% compared with MeCo. Planning time falls by 36% on Move Rope and 32% on Pack Grocery; Make Sandwich and Sort Cubes incur small additional costs of 24 s and 10 s.

Random setting (S1), averaged over 20 random seeds.

Ablation studies

Removing scene similarity makes retrieval more expensive, while removing trajectory similarity lowers reuse reliability. S-Planner transfers valid trajectory segments; continuous planning preserves progress when reuse fails. Selective caching retains efficient plans and avoids propagating redundant steps.

Ablations of scene similarity, trajectory similarity, S-Planner, and continuous planning. Time is in 10² s; tokens are in 10⁴.

Selective caching ablation, averaged over 20 random seeds using DeepSeek-v4-flash.

Performance vs. cache size

Across the tested cache sizes, more cached tasks generally improve success rates and reduce token consumption. Hierarchical retrieval limits expensive trajectory checks to a small candidate set, and the paper observes no planning-time rebound across the five evaluated tasks.
